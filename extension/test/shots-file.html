Learning a language, one small word at a time

Essay · 4 min read

When Meena started her job at the market, she did not need a dictionary so much as a friend who could explain what people really meant. A customer who said the price was a little high was not making a comment about numbers. He was asking, gently and without asking, for a lower one.

Her manager, always careful with words, would say a colleague had been let go when everyone in the room knew the truth. It is a euphemism, a soft way to say something hard, and learning to hear it was part of learning to work here.

Last week a stranger told her the shop went quiet after the rain, and on another day someone wrote that they would recieve the delivery on Friday. Small things like these are where a language is really learned.

Finding it serendipity she had met her neighbour, Meena decided to keep a list.
